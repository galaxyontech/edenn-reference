Eight setup widgets, four comparison tables

Grouped in pairs. Use the A / B toggle on each pair header to flip between the two proposals in place. Everything is clickable, and every widget is drawn at 348px — the real width of the chat pane.

All three layers are live everywhere, sound effects included, as front-end mock only. Worth a note for whoever wires this up: the planner currently drops sfx in two places (agent.py, tools/impls.py), so the backend side is a real change, not a passthrough.

For
Three equal squares say "these are peers, pick any combination" faster than a vertical list does — a stacked list quietly implies an order. Biggest tap targets of the eight, and the one that survives best on a phone.

Layer stack

For
Says the one thing none of the others say: these go on top of your video, not instead of it. The sheets slide out when armed, so the selected set has a physical shape you can read peripherally — and the video sitting above them anchors the whole question.

Sentence tokens

For
Least chrome of the eight and the only one where the agent states a plan rather than administering a questionnaire. Tapping a greyed token switches it on, so accepting the suggestion is one click and changing it is also one click.
Cost
Tap targets inside running text are the weakest affordance here — some people will read it as a sentence and never realise it's editable. Needs the dotted-underline treatment to survive.

Running summary

For
Chips for input, a sentence for confirmation — you never have to simulate the combination in your head, and the three dots give a glanceable count without reading. The summary bar is also the natural place to later say "≈2 min to generate".

Bar meters

For
Bar length is the fastest comparison a person can make — you see "one of these is much busier than the other" before you've read a word. Keeping the word above the bar means the row still works if the value is categorical rather than scalar.
Cost
Four rows of bars starts to look like a spec sheet, and it invites "so is 82 better than 34?" when neither is better. Two or three rows is probably the real limit.

For

Verdict rows

For
The agent does the reasoning instead of dumping two descriptions and leaving you to referee — and each verdict is tied to something about your video ("fits the cuts"), which is the thing a generic music description can never do. The gradient swatch gives each direction an identity that carries into the takes.
Cost
An opinion you disagree with is more annoying than no opinion. And if one column wins every row, you've built a widget that argues with itself about why there are two.
